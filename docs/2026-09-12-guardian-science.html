Indonesian foragers may have sucked on mind-altering nuts 25,000 years ago, research finds

What may be the earliest known evidence for human drug taking has been traced to ancient Indonesian foragers who sucked on mind-altering nuts as long as 25,000 years ago. Analysis of two exceptionally rare skeletons from the Indonesian island of Sulawesi revealed dramatic tooth wear and traces of a psychoactive substance in the individuals’ remaining teeth, suggesting they routinely sucked on hard, abrasive betel nuts. Although little known in western societies, betel nuts are the world’s fourth most widely used psychoactive substance after alcohol, caffeine and nicotine, with an estimated tenth of the global population chewing the seeds. “We’ve found evidence for how this very common drug use habit, which is found throughout a lot of the Asia-Pacific region today, might have evolved,” said Prof Adam Brumm at Griffith University’s Australian Research Centre for Human Evolution in Brisbane. The work began when the team examined skeletal remains of two pre-agricultural foragers unearthed at two different sites on the island. One was dated to about 7,000 years old, the other more ancient at 16,000 to 25,000 to years old. Writing in Science Advances , the researchers describe how both adults had deep, rounded grooves in their teeth. Some went so deep they would have exposed the dental pulp. “I was thinking, what the hell were these people doing? It’s like someone had a drill head shaped like a gobstopper and wore away the inner part of the tooth,” said Brumm. Betel nuts are actually the seeds of the Areca catechu palm, but are misnamed because they are often wrapped in betel vine leaves for chewing. Today, the nuts are typically chewed after being mixed with slaked lime made by grinding burnt shells to form a “quid”. Brumm suspected the bizarre tooth wear was caused by the foragers holding hard, round and abrasive objects in their mouth, but what sort of object? “Imagine you kept a stone in your mouth and you moved it from one part of your mouth to another, and if you did that day after day, hour after hour. Eventually it would wear down your extremely hard dental tissue to form these grooves,” he said. The team wondered whether the grooves were the result of foragers sucking on betel nuts before people realised that mixing the nuts with slaked lime helped release more of the psychoactive drug and intensify the “high”. To test the hunch, the scientists soaked betel nuts in artificial saliva and found that it soon contained enough of the psychoactive compound arecoline to induce a high, albeit a milder one than when the nut is chewed. Analysis of the foragers’ teeth found traces of arecoline in the tissues. “I’ve sucked on a nut as an experiment and it’s a really bitter, astringent taste. It makes your mouth go numb and I think that’s what they were interested in,” Brumm said. “This might have been a traditional remedy for toothache, but if they did it for too long, sucking on the nut wore down their teeth so badly that it actually created much worse dental pain in the long term. These individuals were seemingly caught in a self-perpetuating cycle where they were continually sucking on the nuts to treat the tooth pain caused by this nut-sucking habit.” While humans probably began using mind-altering plants as soon as they encountered them, the oldest traditions of psychoactive drug use are thought to have arisen about 13,000 years ago with fermented drinks such as barley beer. Dr Elisa Guerra-Doce, an associate professor in European prehistory at the University of Valladolid in Spain, said the study provided “direct evidence of the antiquity of human drug use”. “These findings not only represent the earliest known evidence to date of the use of psychoactive plants anywhere in the world, but also demonstrate that the consumption of Areca nuts is a millennia-old tradition dating back to prehistoric times,” she added. 研究发现，印度尼西亚的采集者可能在2.5万年前就吸食过致幻坚果

人类吸毒最早的已知证据，可能来自2.5万年前印度尼西亚的古代觅食者，他们当时会嚼食能改变精神的槟榔。 对印度尼西亚苏拉威西岛上两具极为罕见的骨骼的分析显示，这些古代人牙齿磨损严重，余留的牙齿上留有精神活性物质的痕迹，表明他们经常嚼食坚硬、磨蚀性的槟榔。 虽然槟榔在西方社会鲜为人知，但它是世界上第四大被广泛使用的精神活性物质，仅次于酒精、咖啡因和尼古丁，据估计全球有十分之一的人口嚼食槟榔。 布里斯班格里菲斯大学澳大利亚人类进化研究中心的亚当•布鲁姆教授说：“我们找到了证据，证明这种在当今亚太地区很多地方都非常普遍的吸毒习惯可能是如何演变而来的。” 这项研究始于研究小组对两名农业社会出现前的觅食者的骨骼遗骸的检查，这两具遗骸是在岛上两个不同地点出土的。其中一个可以追溯到大约7000年前，另一个更古老，可以追溯到16000至25000年前。 研究人员在《科学进展》期刊上撰文描述了这两个成年人的牙齿上都有很深的圆形凹槽。有些凹槽很深，会暴露牙髓。布鲁姆说：“我在想，这些人到底在做什么？就像有人拿着一个形状像大块硬糖的钻头，磨掉了牙齿的内部。” 槟榔实际上是槟榔树的种子，但因常被包在槟榔叶中咀嚼而被误称为“槟榔果”。如今，槟榔通常与熟石灰混合后咀嚼，熟石灰由烧过的贝壳研磨而成，形成一种“嚼块”。 布鲁姆怀疑，这种奇特的牙齿磨损是这些觅食者将坚硬、圆润且具有研磨性的物体含在口中造成的，但究竟是什么物体呢？“想象一下，你嘴里含着一块石头，把它从口腔的一个部位移到另一个部位，如果你日复一日、每小时都这样做，最终它会磨损你极其坚硬的牙齿组织，形成这些凹槽，”他说。 研究团队推测，这些凹槽是否是觅食者在人们发现将槟榔与熟石灰混合有助于释放更多精神活性物质并增强“快感”之前吮吸槟榔的结果。 为了验证这一猜想，科学家们将槟榔浸泡在人工唾液中，发现唾液中很快就含有足够的精神活性化合物槟榔碱，足以产生快感，尽管比咀嚼槟榔时的效果要温和一些。对觅食者牙齿的分析在组织中发现了槟榔碱的痕迹。 “我为了做实验吮吸过一颗槟榔，味道非常苦涩，令人发麻。它会让你的嘴巴麻木，我觉得这正是他们感兴趣的地方，”布鲁姆说。 “这或许是一种治疗牙痛的传统偏方，但如果他们吸吮时间过长，坚果会严重磨损牙齿，实际上从长远来看会造成更严重的牙痛。这些人似乎陷入了一种自我延续的循环，他们不断吸吮坚果来治疗由这种吸吮坚果的习惯引起的牙痛。” 虽然人类很可能一遇到致幻植物就开始使用它们，但最古老的精神活性药物使用传统被认为大约在13000年前随着大麦啤酒等发酵饮料的出现而兴起。 西班牙巴利亚多利德大学欧洲史前史副教授埃莉萨·格拉-多塞博士表示，这项研究提供了“人类药物使用古老性的直接证据”。 她补充道：“这些发现不仅代表了迄今为止全世界已知最早的精神活性植物使用证据，还证明了食用槟榔是一项可追溯到史前时代的千年传统。” 对两具骨骼的分析揭示了牙齿磨损和精神活性物质的痕迹，这可能是已知最早的人类吸毒证据。
