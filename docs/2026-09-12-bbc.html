Reform receives record £36m donation from crypto billionaire

Ben Delo pictured in 2022 Reform UK has received a £36m donation from a British cryptocurrency billionaire - a record for a UK political party. Writing in the Daily Telegraph , external , Ben Delo said he was making the move because he wanted a "fair fight and a level playing field". The sum is the biggest single donation ever given to a political party in the UK. Party leader Nigel Farage said he was "honoured and humbled" by the gift. Delo said he was donating £1m a month between now and the latest date Labour could call a general election in 2029 - but that he would give all the money up front in case the government tried to limit mega-donations in future. There are currently no restrictions on the amount of money UK-based donors can give to political parties. But Labour previously introduced a £100,000 cap on donations from British citizens living overseas. Sheffield-born Delo, who has already donated £4m to Reform, has previously said he will move back to the UK from Hong Kong in order to continue his backing for the party. Last year, Delo was given an unconditional pardon by US President Donald Trump over a Bank Secrecy Act violation. In the Telegraph, Delo wrote: "Unlike the people who express mock concern for the health of 'our democracy' while doing everything they can to keep it as a cartel, I want a fair fight and a level playing field. "My conclusion from Reform's recent troubles is that they should spend less time trying to raise funds to compete on equal terms and more time preparing for government." Nigel Farage tells BBC he 'wasn't listening' during foreign donor talks filmed undercover Why Reform UK undercover donations claims are legally complicated Crypto billionaires help Reform cement fundraising lead Reform's fundraising has been under scrutiny with police announcing an investigation over allegations it broke foreign funding laws, following a broadcast by Channel 4 News last week. The party denied any wrongdoing and said it would co-operate with the investigation. Farage is also under investigation by the parliamentary standards commissioner over whether he should have declared a £5m cash gift he received from Thailand-based cryptocurrency investor Christopher Harborne ahead of the 2024 election upon his election as an MP. Farage argues he was under no obligation to register the "purely private" gift, as it was unrelated to his parliamentary or political activities. The Metropolitan Police is also investigating whether payments to Reform in the weeks before the last election complied with donation laws. The police inquiry is looking at donations given directly to the party by Fiona Cottrell, mother of longstanding Farage ally George Cottrell. It is also examining payments from a company controlled by Reform's deputy leader, Richard Tice, which received £1m from Fiona Cottrell in June 2024. The restrictions on donations from Britons living abroad prompted a furious row with Reform and its key donors, who have accused Labour of trying to stymie their party's progress. The government has also sought to prevent overseas voters moving to the UK from giving more than £100,000 for a year after their arrival as part of the Representation of the People Bill, which has its second reading in the Lords on Monday. Last year Harborne gave a single donation of £9m to Reform - which was the biggest single donation to a UK political party by a living person at the time. The Conservatives had received £10m as a legacy but that too has been eclipsed by Delo's latest donation. Responding to the donation, a Labour Party spokesperson said Farage was "still up to his neck in scandal" and "the questions he faces aren't going away - in fact they're piling up". "Reform UK can't buy their way out of questions about sleaze, criminal mates and dodgy finances," they said. "It's time Farage and Reform finally came clean with the public, co-operated fully with the police, and proved they're not just in it for themselves." Liberal Democrat Cabinet Office spokesperson Lisa Smart said Farage wanted to "bring Trump's America to our shores by buying his way into power". "He's trying to get into Number 10 as if it's a stop on a monopoly board," the Liberal Democrat added. "It can't go on like this. Andy Burnham needs to introduce a cap on political donations. Our democracy depends on it."

Reform从加密货币亿万富翁处获得创纪录的3600万英镑捐款

本·德洛摄于2022年 英国改革党收到了一笔来自英国加密货币亿万富翁的3600万英镑捐款——创下英国政党捐款纪录。 本·德洛在《每日电讯报》撰文（外部链接）表示，他之所以这样做，是因为他想要一场“公平的竞争和公平的赛场”。 这笔款项是英国有史以来向政党提供的最大单笔捐款。 该党领袖奈杰尔·法拉奇表示，他对这份馈赠感到“荣幸且谦卑”。 德洛表示，从现在起到工党最晚可能在2029年举行大选之间，他将每月捐款100万英镑——但他会提前一次性支付全部款项，以防政府未来限制巨额捐款。 目前，英国对本土捐款人向政党捐款的金额没有限制。 但工党此前对海外英国公民的捐款设定了10万英镑的上限。 出生于谢菲尔德的德洛已向改革党捐款400万英镑，他此前曾表示将从香港搬回英国，以继续支持该党。 去年，德洛因违反《银行保密法》获得美国总统唐纳德·特朗普的无条件赦免。 在《电讯报》中，德洛写道：“与那些一边假装关心‘我们的民主’健康、一边竭尽全力维持其卡特尔地位的人不同，我想要一场公平的竞争和公平的赛场。” “我从改革党近期的风波中得出的结论是，他们应该少花时间筹款以在同等条件下竞争，多花时间为执政做准备。” 奈杰尔·法拉奇称自己在卧底拍摄的外国捐赠者会谈中“没在听” 为何改革英国党卧底捐赠指控在法律上很复杂 加密货币亿万富翁帮助改革党巩固筹款领先优势 改革党的筹款活动一直受到审视，警方宣布正在调查有关其违反外国资金法律的指控，此前第四频道新闻于上周播出了一则报道。 该党否认有任何不当行为，并表示将配合调查。 法拉奇还正受到议会标准专员的调查，原因是他在2024年大选前从驻泰国的加密货币投资者克里斯托弗·哈伯恩处收到500万英镑现金馈赠，并在当选议员后是否本应申报该馈赠。 法拉奇辩称，他没有义务登记这份“纯私人”馈赠，因为它与他的议会或政治活动无关。 伦敦警察厅还在调查大选前几周向改革党的付款是否符合捐赠法律。 警方的调查正在关注法拉奇的长期盟友乔治·科特雷尔的母亲菲奥娜·科特雷尔直接向该党提供的捐款。 调查同时也在审查由改革党副党魁理查德·泰斯控制的一家公司所支付的款项，该公司于2024年6月从菲奥娜·科特雷尔处收到100万英镑。 对旅居海外的英国人捐款的限制引发了改革党及其主要捐助者的激烈争论，他们指责工党试图阻碍其政党的发展。 政府还试图阻止移居英国的海外选民在抵达后一年内捐款超过10万英镑，这是《人民代表法案》的一部分，该法案将于周一在上议院进行二读。 去年，哈伯恩向改革党一次性捐赠了900万英镑，这是当时在世人士向英国政党捐赠的最大一笔捐款。 保守党曾收到1000万英镑的遗产捐赠，但这一纪录也被德洛的最新捐款超越。 针对这笔捐款，工党发言人表示，法拉奇“仍深陷丑闻”，“他面临的问题不会消失——事实上，问题正在越积越多”。 “改革英国党无法用金钱摆脱关于贪腐、犯罪同伙和可疑财务的问题，”他们说，“法拉奇和改革党是时候向公众坦白，全面配合警方，并证明他们不仅仅是为了一己私利。” 自由民主党内阁办公室发言人丽莎·斯马特表示，法拉奇想“通过花钱买权，把特朗普的美国带到我们的海岸”。 “他试图进入唐宁街10号，就好像那是大富翁棋盘上的一站，”这位自由民主党人补充道，“不能再这样下去了。安迪·伯纳姆需要引入政治捐款上限。我们的民主有赖于此。”
