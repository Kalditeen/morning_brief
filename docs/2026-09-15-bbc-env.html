US scraps limits on emissions from coal and gas power plants

Rules aimed at limiting pollution from coal and gas power plants are being scrapped, the head of the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has announced. The agency also said it would take steps to prevent future administrations from regulating power plant emissions. The EPA said that getting rid of the majority of the climate rules from President Joe Biden's era would save $310bn (£223bn) and reduce energy prices. But environmental groups warned it would come at a huge cost to Americans' health and the planet. The EPA's move is expected to face immediate legal challenges. "The reality is that America produces energy better and cleaner than anywhere else in the world, and our power plants should not be unfairly targeted," the head of the EPA, Lee Zeldin, said on Monday. Fossil fuels, which include coal and natural gas, are by far the largest driver of global climate change, according to the United Nations. The EPA said in a statement that it found the Biden-era rules on power plant emissions had "exceeded the agency's authority". The agency said the regulations required "control technologies that are not adequately demonstrated, effectively forcing plants to retire rather than setting standards they could actually meet". The EPA is also seeking to get rid of all other greenhouse gas rules for the power sector. The changes, which will undergo public scrutiny, were proposed last year by Zeldin. He said the new rules would "ensure affordable and reliable energy supplies and drive down the costs of transportation, heating, utilities, farming, and manufacturing while boosting our national security". The Biden-era regulations called for a major reduction in carbon emissions from power plants, as well as vehicles and industrial facilities. It was estimated that the measures would stop 4,500 premature deaths annually. But the EPA said on Monday: "Any potential public health harms are too uncertain, conjectural, remote, and convoluted to tie specifically to the US power sector." Zeldin told reporters that safeguards against "hazardous air pollutants" would remain in place "to safeguard human health". Nathaniel Keohane, president of the Center for Climate and Energy Solutions – an independent environmental organisation – described the move as "a giant step backward". "In repealing carbon pollution standards for power plants, EPA has ignored sound science and economics and abdicated its responsibility to protect public health and welfare," Keohane added. The US is currently the second-biggest emitter of planet-warming greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide, behind China. And the power sector makes up about a quarter of US emissions, at roughly 1.5 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide-equivalent each year, according to the latest available estimates. That means the US power sector emissions are higher than the total emissions of all but a handful of countries. US power sector emissions have been generally falling over the past couple of decades, albeit with a slight uptick last year, as coal use has fallen. But Trump has long argued for a resurgence in coal power and has regularly criticised renewables such as wind and solar. The precise consequences of today's announcement for US emissions - and global climate change - are uncertain. Some researchers question whether coal will ever make the resurgence that Trump hopes for. They point to the sharp falls in costs of many clean technologies such as solar power over the past decade. In 2022, the EPA lost some of its powers to limit greenhouse gas emissions when the Supreme Court ruled in favour of 19 states and some of the nation's largest coal companies, which were worried about the economic cost of the policy and argued the agency did not have the authority to limit emissions across whole states. Monday's announcement is the latest move by President Donald Trump's administration to walk back policies designed to combat global warming and climate change. In February, the EPA reversed a landmark Obama-era scientific ruling, which held that greenhouse gases were a threat to public health. This was challenged by several states and local governments. 美国取消燃煤和燃气发电厂的排放限制

旨在限制燃煤和燃气电厂污染的规则正在被废除，美国环境保护署（EPA）署长宣布。 该机构还表示，将采取措施防止未来政府监管电厂排放。 EPA表示，废除拜登总统时代的大部分气候规则将节省3100亿美元（2230亿英镑）并降低能源价格。 但环保组织警告称，这将给美国人的健康和地球带来巨大代价。EPA的这一举措预计将立即面临法律挑战。 “现实是，美国生产能源的方式比世界上任何地方都更好、更清洁，我们的电厂不应受到不公平的针对，”EPA署长李·泽尔丁周一表示。 根据联合国的说法，化石燃料——包括煤炭和天然气——是迄今为止全球气候变化的最大驱动因素。 EPA在一份声明中表示，它发现拜登时代关于电厂排放的规则“超出了该机构的权限”。 该机构表示，这些法规要求“未经充分验证的控制技术，实际上迫使电厂退役，而不是设定它们能够真正达到的标准”。 EPA还寻求废除针对电力行业的所有其他温室气体规则。 这些变更将接受公众审查，由泽尔丁去年提出。 他说，新规将“确保负担得起且可靠的能源供应，降低交通、供暖、公用事业、农业和制造业的成本，同时增强我们的国家安全”。 拜登时期的法规要求大幅减少发电厂以及车辆和工业设施的碳排放。据估计，这些措施每年可避免4500人过早死亡。 但美国环保署周一表示：“任何潜在的公卫危害都过于不确定、推测性、遥远且复杂，无法具体归因于美国电力行业。” 泽尔丁告诉记者，针对“危险空气污染物”的防护措施将继续保留，“以保障人类健康”。 独立环境组织“气候与能源解决方案中心”主席纳撒尼尔·基奥汉称此举是“巨大的倒退”。 基奥汉补充说：“废除发电厂碳污染标准，环保署忽视了严谨的科学和经济学，并放弃了其保护公众健康和福祉的责任。” 美国目前是仅次于中国的第二大温室气体排放国，这些气体包括二氧化碳等导致全球变暖的气体。 根据可获得的最新估计，美国电力行业约占全国排放量的四分之一，每年约排放15亿吨二氧化碳当量。 这意味着美国电力行业的排放量高于除少数几个国家外的所有国家的总排放量。 过去几十年来，随着煤炭使用量下降，美国电力行业的排放量总体呈下降趋势，尽管去年略有上升。 但特朗普一直主张振兴煤电，并经常批评风能和太阳能等可再生能源。 今天的声明对美国排放量以及全球气候变化的确切影响尚不确定。 一些研究人员质疑煤炭是否会像特朗普所希望的那样复苏。他们指出，过去十年中，太阳能等许多清洁技术的成本大幅下降。 2022年，美国环保署限制温室气体排放的部分权力被削弱，当时最高法院裁定19个州和全国一些大型煤炭公司胜诉，这些州和公司担心该政策的经济成本，并主张环保署无权在整州范围内限制排放。 周一的声明是唐纳德·特朗普总统政府撤销旨在应对全球变暖和气候变化政策的最新举措。 今年2月，环保署推翻了一项具有里程碑意义的奥巴马时代科学裁定，该裁定认为温室气体对公众健康构成威胁。这一决定遭到多个州和地方政府的质疑。 清洁空气如何加速全球变暖——并揭示一个更大的问题 化石燃料排放再次上升——但可再生能源的繁荣为气候带来希望 气候变化简易指南
